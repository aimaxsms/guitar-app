Chord Theory

A chord is built by stacking specific intervals on top of a root note. A major chord is Root + Major 3rd (4 semitones) + Perfect 5th (7 semitones). A minor chord is Root + Minor 3rd (3 semitones) + Perfect 5th (7 semitones). The only difference between a major and minor chord with the same root is that one note — the 3rd — moves down a single semitone.

Keys

A song's "key" is the scale its chords and melody are built from — the same root and major/minor choice you just picked above also defines a key. Once you know a song's key, you know the 7 chords most likely to show up in it, built by stacking a 3rd and 5th on top of each note of that key's scale. For example:

Key of C major: C – Dm – Em – F – G – Am – B°
Key of G major: G – Am – Bm – C – D – Em – F#°
Key of E minor: Em – F#° – G – Am – Bm – C – D

Music Theory

A step-by-step course from the 12 notes to modes, made for guitar. Each lesson has a picture of the neck, sounds you can play, and a quick quiz. Your progress is saved on this device.

Scales

A scale is a set of notes built from a specific pattern of intervals above a root. Pentatonic scales (5 notes) are the easiest to improvise with — minor pentatonic is the backbone of blues and rock soloing. The 7-note scales and modes below shift the mood by changing which notes are flattened or sharpened relative to the major scale.

Live Chords

Listens to your guitar in real time and guesses the chord you're playing — entirely on this device, no recognition service, no API key, no upload. Play through a song and it builds up the chord progression as you go. Accuracy depends on a clean, mostly-solo guitar signal; heavy background noise or a full band mix will confuse it.
